Home / Atlas

From home, with one degree.

Two universal strategies: Path A — finish your MD at home, then enter residency abroad. Path B — complete residency and your board at home, then move abroad as a specialist or fellow. And the question everyone asks too late: can you actually choose your specialty there?

00 / The home-base strategy

It starts in your home country — always.

Whatever you choose, your home-country degree is the launchpad. From it you get exactly two strategies, and the whole atlas is a study of which one to run:

Path A — leave early

Fresh MD → residency abroad

Graduate at home, then sit the destination's exams (USMLE, UKMLA, AMC, NZREX, Kenntnisprüfung…) and enter residency abroad. Higher upside, higher cost: exam fees, visas, and competitive gates — but you train inside the system you want to stay in. Start preparing in your clinical years.

Path B — leave senior

Home board → abroad as specialist/fellow

Complete residency and your national board at home, then move: the Gulf accepts your board almost immediately (licensing exams only); the UK/Ireland assess it via portfolio routes; Germany credits it partially; Australia/NZ run it through specialist comparability; the US/Canada don't recognise it. Best when home training is strong or you need income fast.

The hybrid

Home residency → targeted fellowship abroad

Board at home, then do a 1–2 year clinical fellowship in the US, UK, Germany or the Gulf — often the smoothest way to work abroad, build the network, and convert a fellowship into a job offer. See the fellowship catalog for the menu.

01 / The seven doorways

The IMG routes, step by step.

The detailed mechanisms for the destinations that matter most — exams, registrations, visas, budgets, timing.

Route 01

United States (ERAS Match)

ECFMG certification → USMLE Step 1 (P/F) + Step 2 CK (score is king) + Step 3 (often after match) → US clinical rotations (USCE) → research → ERAS application (Sep of final MD year) → Match (March). Visas: J-1 (ECFMG-sponsored) or H-1B (program-dependent). Budget: ~$6–10k in exam/application fees; USCE costs extra.

When to startMED 3–4: Steps 1+2; final year: ERAS.
Reality checkRealistic target for IM/FM/peds/neuro/path/anesth/rad; very hard for surgical subs — needs research years.
Route 02

Australia (AMC Standard + colleges)

EPIC primary-source verification → OET (B all) / IELTS Academic (7.0 each band) → AMC CAT MCQ (can be sat overseas) → AMC Clinical exam (in AU) or 6–12-month Workplace-Based Assessment at an accredited hospital → AMC Certificate → AHPRA provisional registration → 12 months supervised practice (min. 47 weeks) → general registration → RMO/registrar posts (rural / Area of Need easiest) → apply to a specialty college (RACS SET, RACP, RACGP…). Already board-certified? SIMG Specialist Pathway: AMC + college comparability assessment, often with supervised practice and/or exams. Visas: 482/186 employer-sponsored or 189/190 skilled — doctors are prioritised. Realistic total ~2–3 years end-to-end; budget ~A$15–25k.

When to startAfter graduation: AMC CAT (can be sat overseas); English anytime.
Reality checkNo US-style national match, but college selection is just as competitive: cardiothoracic SET is a single national merit selection — published offers: 13 (2019), 6 (2021), 7 (2022), 6 (2023), 9 (2024), 8 (2025), max 3 attempts from the 2026 round; cardiology = RACP basic training + a state advanced-training match (PR/citizenship preferred). GP, psychiatry and rural medicine are the IMG-friendly doors.
Route 03

New Zealand (NZREX → colleges)

English first: IELTS Academic 7.5 / OET B (all components) → pass a prerequisite exam (USMLE Step 1+2, AMC MCQ, or PLAB 1 — free-rides on prep you'd do anyway) → MCNZ NZREX Clinical (held in Auckland) → you MUST then secure a PGY1/house-officer post to obtain provisional registration (apply directly to Te Whatu Ora / Health NZ hospitals) → supervised practice → general registration → RMO/house-officer roles → specialty training via Australasian colleges (RACP, RANZCP, RNZCGP…). Board-certified: MCNZ vocational registration via college assessment. Visa: Green List Tier 1 — specialists, GPs and even RMOs are on the Straight to Residence list.

When to startAfter graduation — prerequisite exam overlaps US/AMC prep.
Reality checkSmall market — GP, psychiatry, rural and old-age care are the realistic entry points. Once registered (~2+ yrs), NZ becomes a Competent Authority for Australia — a proven bridge.
Route 04

United Kingdom (UKMLA route)

OET/IELTS → GMC registration → UKMLA (successor to PLAB) → Foundation Programme application (Oriel) or trust-grade SHO posts → core training → specialty training → CCT. Visa: Health & Care Worker visa (fast, no labour-market test).

When to startMED 4: UKMLA; after graduation: Foundation.
Reality checkA classic IMG route; competitive Foundation but trust-grade bridging works.
Route 05

Germany (Approbation route)

German B2 (then C1 for most specialties) → statement of comparability → Approbation (licence) after Kenntnisprüfung (skills exam) → direct applications for Assistenzarzt posts. Visa: §16d doctor's visa (fast-track).

When to startStart German NOW (MED 2–3) so B2 is ready by graduation.
Reality checkNo match lottery; you interview directly with hospitals. Most open European system.
Route 06

Saudi Arabia (Saudi Board)

SCFHS registration via Mumaris → dataflow verification → Saudi Licensing Exam (SLE) → residency application (Saudi Board programs, 4–5 yr) or direct specialist classification for board-certified doctors. Arabic required for patient care roles.

When to startApply in final MD year; SLE after graduation.
Reality checkTraining + tax-free salary + proximity. Internationally trained doctors are a known quantity.
Route 07

UAE / Qatar (direct + residency)

DHA (Dubai), HAAD/DoH (Abu Dhabi), QCHP (Qatar) licensing: primary-source verification + exam (depending on pathway) + Arabic for some roles. Board-certified specialists can move directly; residents can enter DHA/DoH training tracks.

When to startAfter graduation (specialist route) or during residency (transfer).
Reality checkBest end-destination for Gulf income; often combined with KSA/Germany/UK training.

Path A, Path B, and who controls your specialty.

Your home base plus all 15 destinations in one matrix. Path A = residency entry as a fresh MD. Path B = specialist move after a home-country board. Mechanism = how specialty choice works there.

Many IMGs start on a temporary Berufserlaubnis while full recognition processes. No match, no lottery. No true 'attending' shortcut. Direct / free choice
Direct application — genuinely free choice: you start your chosen specialty wherever you are hired. Some fields saturated in big cities (derm, radiology), shortage fields hire anyone (psych, GP, anesthesia, path, ICU).
🇨🇭 Switzerland Direct applications for Assistenzarzt posts after FMH-recognized degree + language; non-EU work-permit quota is tiny. Home-country board not recognized — realistic only after a European (e.g., German) Facharzt, then comparative assessment. Direct — quota-gated
Direct application, but quota-gated for non-EU — de facto closed until you hold EU/CH credentials.
🇫🇷 France ECNi national ranking exam — every candidate ranked, picks specialty+city in rank order. Non-EU: EVC quota (tiny) + fluent French. Home-country board not recognized; EVC/redo of DES training. National ranking exam
Exam-rank system: your rank decides your choice — you do not simply choose.
🇳🇱 Netherlands BIG registration + near-native Dutch (NT2/B2+); ANIOS service years → apply to 'opleiding' (residency) openings per program. Specialist recognition via equivalence assessment (often with a supervised period); language decisive. Direct — language-gated
Direct per-program application — competitive; you can pursue your chosen specialty but Dutch fluency gates everything.
🇸🇪 Sweden / 🇳🇴 Norway Language (Sfi/C1 or Norwegian) + authority approval (Socialstyrelsen/Helsedir) → direct applications to ST (specialist training) posts; LIS1 internship first in Norway. Recognition of specialist quals + supplementary requirements; shortage specialties practical. Direct — shortage-friendly
Direct application to ST posts — shortage specialties easy (psych, GP, geriatrics, radiology), popular ones competitive.

SIMG Specialist Pathway: AMC + college comparability assessment (e.g., RACP, RACS) — usually 1–2 yrs supervised practice and sometimes college exams → specialist registration. College-based competitive selection — NOT free choice and NOT one national match: RACS SET = single national merit selection (cardiothoracic published offers 2019–2025: 13, 6, 7, 6, 9, 8 — max 3 eligible attempts from the 2026 round; neurosurgery similar scarcity); cardiology = RACP basic training + state advanced-training MATCH (e.g., the PMCV Victorian match; PR/citizenship ranked first); GP (RACGP), psychiatry, rural = the IMG-accessible doors.

College selection in a small system — GP (RNZCGP), psychiatry, rural and old-age care are the realistic choices; popular specialties take few trainees.
The one-line summary: Direct-application freedom = your home country, Germany, Switzerland, the Gulf, the Nordics and the Netherlands (language-gated). Match-or-competitive = the US (NRMP), Canada (CaRMS — closed without PR), UK/Ireland (national selection), France (exam rank), and Australia/NZ where colleges select and surgical intakes are single digits. The only destinations where "attending after your home board" is immediate: the Gulf (licensing exams only). Everywhere else either re-trains you (Germany), assesses you (UK/Ireland portfolio route, Australia's SIMG pathway, NZ vocational registration), or excludes you (US).
03 / The mechanism types

Three ways a country picks your specialty for you.

Type 1 — National match

You rank them, they rank you

US (NRMP), Canada (CaRMS), UK (Oriel national selection), France (ECNi rank). Your specialty is effectively fixed the day you submit your application. As an IMG the realistic US list is IM / FM / peds / neuro / path / anesthesia / radiology; surgical fields are single-digit territory.

Type 2 — College selection

Service years first, then the gate

Australia & New Zealand (colleges), Ireland. No central match, but no free choice either: you do unaccredited service years, then compete for college training posts. RACS cardiothoracic surgery made 6–13 offers per year nationally across 2019–2025. GP, psychiatry and rural medicine are the accessible doors.

Type 3 — Free direct application

Hired = training starts

Your home country, Germany, Switzerland, the Gulf, the Netherlands and the Nordics. You apply straight to hospitals for the specialty you want; getting hired IS the selection. The gates are language (Germany, NL, Nordics), saturation (derm/radiology in big cities), or — for the Gulf — simply passing licensing exams.

04 / Worked examples

Can you do the big surgical dreams there?

Scenario testingthe popular fields vs the world
Neurosurgery —Germany is the realistic Western door (direct Assistenzarzt applications in Neurochirurgie). Australia/NZ = RACS national selection, ~10–15 places/yr across both countries, PR near-mandatory. US = single-digit IMG places. Gulf = Saudi Board / DHA programs, IMG-friendly.
Cardiology —US: match IM first (realistic), then win the cardiology fellowship — the strongest US play for an IMG. Australia: RACP basic training + state advanced-training match (PR ranked first). Germany: direct Kardiologie Facharzt path. UK: Oriel ST3 selection, very competitive. Gulf: direct training, or attending after your home board.
Interventional Radiology —US: integrated-IR match is hard for IMGs — DR then ESIR bridge is easier. Germany: Radiologie + interventionelle track, direct application. Australia: RANZCR training then IR (competitive). Gulf: growing IR programs with good IMG acceptance.
Anesthesiology —Among the most IMG-matchable well-paid US fields. Australia: ANZCA selection after service years (moderate). Germany/UK/Gulf: open via direct or selection-based entry.
Family medicine / GP / psychiatry / rural —The universal IMG door in every single country: US (FM/psych match), UK (GP training), Germany (Allgemeinmedizin), Australia (RACGP), NZ (RNZCGP), Gulf (FM expansion). If you ever need the fastest exit ticket from home, this is the lever everywhere.
