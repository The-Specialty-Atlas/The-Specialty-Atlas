Fifteen destinations, one home base.

From Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 buildout to New Zealand's Green List: how long training takes, what residents and attendings earn, how feasible each door is with a home-country MD, and the verdict on each. Your home country itself is covered on the Routes page as the launchpad.

01 / First, the hard truth

The passport reality check

A home-country passport opens most doors — but not all of them, and not equally. Read this before falling in love with any destination.

Canada — effectively CLOSED for now.

CaRMS residency requires Canadian permanent residency or citizenship. As a foreign citizen with no PR, you cannot apply to Canadian residency directly. Some IMGs get Canadian PR later via Express Entry / PNP once they are experienced specialists — but Canadian residency itself is not an option for you today. Treat Canada as a 'post-training emigration target', not a training destination.

US — open but uphill.

Non-US IMGs matched 58.0% in the 2025 Match, but competitive specialties take very few IMGs (neurosurgery: single digits per year, mostly US-IMG or research-heavy candidates). The realistic US lane: Step 1 (P/F) + Step 2 CK (score matters) + US clinical experience + research + strong LoRs. J-1 visa available; H-1B for some programs; both need ECFMG certification. It is a 2-year grind from MED 2, but it is genuinely doable for mid-competitive specialties (IM, FM, peds, neuro, path, anesthesia, radiology) and possible-but-very-hard for surgical subspecialties.

Gulf (KSA/UAE/Qatar) — OPEN and welcoming.

Internationally trained physicians are a long-established community in Gulf hospitals. SCFHS (Saudi) runs residency through the Saudi Board — seats are increasingly Saudized but non-Saudi residents are still admitted, especially in shortage fields; UAE (DHA/HAAD) and Qatar (QCHP/HMC) accept international graduates into their residency/fellowship tracks and consultant market. Tax-free income; Arabic speakers hold an advantage. The Gulf is your lowest-friction high-income option.

UK — open via UKMLA (ex-PLAB).

You need GMC registration: pass OET/IELTS + UKMLA (PLAB is now the UKMLA), then compete for Foundation Programme jobs (competitive for IMGs) or enter via trust-grade posts. UK training is long and NHS pay is modest vs the US/Gulf, but the UK is a springboard (CCT is recognised in the Gulf and many countries).

Ireland — open but competitive.

Irish Medical Council registration + internship scheme (IMC 'intern year' places are competitive for non-EU grads). Good pay-to-cost balance; pathway to the Gulf is strong.

Germany — the most IMG-friendly big system.

~60,000+ foreign-trained doctors work in Germany. Route: German B2→C1 + Approbation (recognition of your home-country MD) + Kenntnisprüfung; then paid residency (Weiterbildung) in your specialty. No match lottery — you apply directly to hospitals. Very realistic for international grads; language is the only real barrier (12–18 months of study).

France — hard.

Non-EU graduates need French + 'Épreuves de vérification des connaissances' (EVC) with very few places. Generally not recommended as a primary plan.

Switzerland — elite but tiny quota.

Non-EU/EFTA physicians need a work permit; cantons admit only a small quota (2,000+/yr nationally for all professions; doctors get a share). Possible with strong CV, but treat as a long shot until you have a European registration.

Netherlands/Scandinavia — language-locked.

Excellent pay and conditions, but you need near-native Dutch or a Scandinavian language plus BIG registration (NL). Realistic only if you commit to 1–2 years of language study.

Australia & New Zealand — genuinely open (no nationality quota).

Both run exam-based registration for international graduates (AMC / NZREX) rather than the US-style match — reachable with no nationality quota, and doctors sit on priority visa lists (NZ Green List = Straight to Residence). The catch is different: specialty training is run by colleges that select competitively, so surgical/cardiology dreams typically need service years, a strong CV and often PR. GP, psychiatry, rural medicine and radiology are the realistic doors — and NZ registration doubles as a fast lane into Australia.

03 / The dossiers

All fifteen, in full.

🇸🇦Saudi Arabia

Training route

Saudi Board (SCFHS) residencies 4–5 yrs; seats partly non-Saudi; Mumaris platform; SLE exam.

Resident pay

SAR 8–15k/mo ($25–48k/yr) tax-free

Attending pay

Specialist SAR 25–50k/mo · Consultant SAR 40–70k/mo ($130–225k/yr)

IMG status

Good — home-country MDs well accepted; Arabic advantage; Vision 2030 expanding seats (needs ~40k more doctors by 2030)

Culture & lifestyle

48–60h/wk; structured training; big tertiary centres (KFSH&RC, King Saud) doing world-class surgery

AI adoption

Rapid AI investment (SEHA, NUPCO digitalisation); among the region's most advanced

Verdict: Best overall Gulf package for training + savings + proximity. Tax-free compounding is powerful.

🇦🇪UAE

Training route

Residency/fellowship via DHA/HAAD/DoH tracks; also hires board-certified specialists directly.

Resident pay

AED 25–40k/mo ($82–130k/yr) tax-free (private programs)

Attending pay

Specialist AED 40–70k/mo · Consultant AED 60–120k+/mo ($200–390k/yr)

IMG status

Very good — large, established expat medical community; Golden Visa for specialists (10-yr residency)

Culture & lifestyle

45–55h/wk; modern facilities (Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi, Mediclinic, Moorfields Dubai); lifestyle best in region

AI adoption

Among the most AI-forward health systems in the Middle East (DAMAN, M42 AI radiology platform)

Verdict: Best for: lifestyle + luxury pay as a board-certified specialist. Smaller residency training footprint than KSA — many go 'KSA/Germany for training, UAE for the money'.

🇶🇦Qatar

Training route

HMC residency programs (ACGME-I accredited) — international grads accepted; QCHP licensing.

Resident pay

QAR 15–25k/mo ($4–7k/mo) tax-free

Attending pay

Consultants QAR 60–80k+/mo private ($200–320k/yr)

IMG status

Good; HMC is a major regional training hub with Sidra (women's/children's)

Culture & lifestyle

45–55h/wk; small country, big facilities, strong academics

AI adoption

High adoption; HMC partnering on AI diagnostics

Verdict: Strong mid-size option; excellent for peds/women's health training.

🇰🇼Kuwait

Training route

KIMS residency programs; smaller system.

Resident pay

KWD ~1.2–2k/mo (tax-free)

Attending pay

Consultant KWD 4–7k/mo (~$13–23k/mo)

IMG status

Good for IMGs; fewer training seats

Culture & lifestyle

Short hours; slower clinical volume

AI adoption

Moderate

Verdict: Great pay-to-effort ratio for specialists; training options thinner than KSA/UAE.

🇺🇸United States

Training route

ACGME residency 3–7 yrs; Match (ERAS); fellowship 1–3 yrs.

Resident pay

PGY-1 $68,166 → PGY-7 $89,187 (AAMC 2025)

Attending pay

Median ~$350–450k overall; surgical/IR/procedural $500–900k+

IMG status

Non-US IMG match 58.0% (2025); competitive specialties single-digit IMG seats; needs USMLE + USCE + research

Culture & lifestyle

80h/wk cap; high volume; litigation-aware; midlevels (PAs/NPs) encroaching

AI adoption

Fastest AI adoption + 1,000+ FDA-cleared tools; teleradiology and AI scribes mainstream

Verdict: The gold standard for training, income ceiling, and innovation — at the price of the hardest entry and the most debt/competition.

🇨🇦Canada

Training route

RCPSC residency via CaRMS; IMG stream requires PR/citizenship.

Resident pay

PGY-1 CA$73,367 (Ontario) → PGY-5 CA$98,567

Attending pay

CA$280–450k typical; surgical CA$400–550k

IMG status

CLOSED to you today (no PR). Revisit after training via Express Entry/PNP.

Culture & lifestyle

60–75h/wk; high standard; slower pace than US

AI adoption

Moderate adoption; strong academic centres

Verdict: Not a training option for a foreign citizen without PR — a post-specialisation emigration dream.

🇬🇧United Kingdom

Training route

Foundation (2 yr) → core (2–3 yr) → specialty registrar (4–6 yr) → CCT; IMG route via UKMLA.

Resident pay

FY1 £38,831 · ST3–5 £65,048 · ST6–8 £73,992 (2025/26)

Attending pay

Consultant £109,725–145,478 (~$143–190k); private top-ups possible

IMG status

Open — UKMLA + OET/IELTS + Foundation application; trust-grade bridging jobs common

Culture & lifestyle

48h/wk contract (EWTD); rota-heavy; NHS underfunding; strikes over pay

AI adoption

NHS AI Lab active; imaging AI scaling; still slower than US

Verdict: Reliable, respectable, IMG-friendly route with a globally recognised CCT — but modest pay and long training. Excellent springboard to the Gulf/Australia.

🇮🇪Ireland

Training route

Internship (1 yr) → basic specialist training (2–3 yr) → higher specialist training (4–5 yr); IMC registration.

Resident pay

Intern ~€37k → registrar ~€58–75k

Attending pay

Consultant €150–220k public (+private)

IMG status

Open but internship places competitive for non-EU; strong international medical ties

Culture & lifestyle

48h/wk; European-style training with English language

AI adoption

Growing (HSE digital strategy)

Verdict: Attractive English-language Europe option; CCT-equivalent recognised in the Gulf.

🇦🇺Australia

Training route

No IMG internship (PGY1 is reserved for local graduates). AMC Standard Pathway: CAT MCQ exam → AMC Clinical (in AU) or 6-month Workplace-Based Assessment → AHPRA general registration after ~12 months supervised → RMO/service-registrar years (rural / Area of Need easiest) → specialty college training (RACS SET, RACP, RACGP, ACEM, ANZCA, RANZCR…).

Resident pay

RMO A$90–130k · registrar A$130–190k (incl. penalties, ≈$60–125k USD)

Attending pay

Public consultant A$250–400k · private/procedural A$400–700k+ (≈$165–455k USD)

IMG status

Open but exam-gated: AMC exams for a home-country MD (no Competent-Authority shortcut); the real gate is college selection (PR/citizenship strongly preferred); 10-year Medicare moratorium unless specialist-recognised; budget A$15–25k for the pathway

Culture & lifestyle

38–50h/wk public system; excellent work-life and supervision; rural posts easiest to enter; strong unionised conditions

AI adoption

High adoption; sophisticated digital health; big clinical-trials sector

Verdict: Superb lifestyle + pay + English. Realistic doors: GP (RACGP), psychiatry, rural medicine, or the SIMG Specialist Pathway if you arrive board-certified. Competitive dreams (surgery, cardiology) face tiny national intakes (e.g., cardiothoracic SET 6–13 offers/yr AU-wide).

🇳🇿New Zealand

Training route

MCNZ registration for non-comparable qualifications: NZREX Clinical exam + English (IELTS/OET) → provisional registration → supervised practice → general registration → RMO/house-officer roles via direct application to Health NZ hospitals → specialty training via the Australasian colleges (RACP, RANZCP, RACS-NZ) or the NZ GP college (RNZCGP).

Resident pay

House officer NZ$80–150k · registrar NZ$96–180k (incl. penalties, ≈$48–110k USD)

Attending pay

SMO (specialist) NZ$180–350k+ (≈$110–215k USD; private adds more)

IMG status

Open: NZREX is the gate; doctors are on the Green List Tier 1 (Straight to Residence visa); small market — shortage fields (GP, psychiatry, rural, old-age care) are where jobs are

Culture & lifestyle

40–50h/wk; 6 weeks annual leave; genuinely excellent balance

AI adoption

Moderate-high adoption; small but digital-forward health system

Verdict: The easiest English-speaking full-package route after exams, and a smart bridge: NZ registration (2+ yrs) makes you a Competent Authority for Australia — many doctors do NZ first, AU second.

🇩🇪Germany

Training route

Weiterbildung (residency) 5–6 yrs by specialty, after Approbation + Kenntnisprüfung; direct hospital applications — no Match.

Resident pay

Assistenzarzt €62.4k → €76.8k (yr 5)

Attending pay

Facharzt €79–102k · Oberarzt €102–126k · Chefarzt €144–300k+

IMG status

Most open big system: ~60k foreign doctors; visa for doctors; language B2/C1 the gate

Culture & lifestyle

40–48h/wk; strong structure; document-heavy; hierarchy

AI adoption

Solid adoption (university hospitals); conservative in rural areas

Verdict: Your most realistic 'train in Europe' option — no lottery, paid training, EU Facharzt that the Gulf loves. Learn German now and it's yours.

🇨🇭Switzerland

Training route

Assistenzarzt positions after FMH-recognised degree; non-EU quota ~small; German/French/Italian language.

Resident pay

CHF 80–120k

Attending pay

Specialist CHF 140–220k · Chief CHF 250–500k+

IMG status

Hard for non-EU; realistic only after European board certification (Facharzt) — then competitive

Culture & lifestyle

50–60h/wk; superb resources; highest European pay

AI adoption

Early adopter (university hospitals)

Verdict: Best European income, but the door opens AFTER you hold a European Facharzt — a second-step target.

🇫🇷France

Training route

DES residency after ECN/ECNi (national ranking) — non-EU via EVC quota (tiny).

Resident pay

Internat €26–38k (low)

Attending pay

Hospitalier €80–130k (+private)

IMG status

Hard for non-EU without French citizenship/ties; EVC places ~500/yr vs thousands of applicants

Culture & lifestyle

48h/wk; strong public system; unionised

AI adoption

Moderate; Hôpitaux de Paris AI projects

Verdict: Skip as primary plan unless you have strong French + family ties.

🇳🇱Netherlands

Training route

ANIOS (pre-residency) → opleiding (residency) 4–6 yrs; BIG registration + near-native Dutch (NT2/B2+).

Resident pay

€56–75k

Attending pay

Specialist €110–180k

IMG status

Possible with Dutch fluency; non-EU grads face credential review

Culture & lifestyle

40–48h/wk; excellent work-life; GP-heavy system

AI adoption

Advanced digital health infrastructure

Verdict: Great life, high language bar — a niche plan for the Dutch-fluent.

🇸🇪Sweden / Norway

Training route

Residency (ST) 5 yrs; requires Swedish (Sfi/C1) + Socialstyrelsen/Helsedir approval.

Resident pay

SEK ~45–55k/mo (≈$4.3–5.3k) · NOK ~700k–800k/yr

Attending pay

Specialist ~$120–170k; strong pension

IMG status

Open for IMGs with language; long bureaucracy

Culture & lifestyle

40h/wk; the most balanced medicine in the world

AI adoption

High digital maturity

Verdict: Best lifestyle in Europe; the language investment is the price.
